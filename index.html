Cutting help & estimate details

Paper outlines shows half templates on visibly folded fabric (two layers, or four for fourfold Buba). The cutting plan still reserves the full fabric needed for each fold. Opened-out fabric shows the full cut shape. Fold at the purple edge before cutting. Use arrow keys to move a focused piece (Shift = 1 inch). Zoom to inspect or drag precisely; Fit all shows the whole layout. Shaded blocks reserve cutting room, so overlaps are flagged even if the outlines do not touch. Automatic layout keeps grain fixed; manual rotation is unrestricted, so check grain, stretch and fold direction before cutting. The estimate reserves 1 inch for selvedges and ½ inch between blocks, adds your optional extra percentage and rounds to ¼ yard. Extra is a purchasing buffer for shrinkage, mistakes or matching prints; it does not enlarge the pattern. Set 0% if you do not want that buffer. Blank cloth below the pieces is the unused part of the length you entered, not extra added to the pattern. Recalculate for different material widths. Haberdashery and undrafted lining are extra; allow more for shrinkage or matched prints.

FROM SCREEN TO SEWING TABLE

Print. Tape. Test fit.

  1. Bra presets and family sizes. US band/cup presets fill estimated bust, underbust and cup dimensions. Advanced cup adjustments are optional; brands and breast shape vary. Child/youth sizes are height-based estimates, with custom measurements available. Corsets and bra presets are adult options. The garment model is a live style illustration, not a 3D fit or fabric simulation. Grey garments are a neutral background outfit.
  2. Measure first. Select a reference size or enter body measurements. Custom mode unlocks all fields. Advanced body lengths are estimated in presets; replace them for personal fit.
  3. Print at actual size. Download the PDF, choose A4 portrait, 100% / actual size, and turn off “fit to page.” The first page has a 50 × 50 mm test square. Measure it before printing the rest.
  4. Assemble the shared layout. Pieces are arranged together to save sheets. Tiles have a borderless layout, a default 5 mm overlap only at connections, and row/column labels. Place the next sheet’s paper edge on the previous sheet’s dashed connection guide; match C/R labels, arrows and repeated pattern lines. Follow the shared assembly map in your PDF or open A4 assembly in the app; zoom in to see how the pieces cross sheet boundaries. Cut individual pieces after taping.
  5. Cut and sew. Solid lines include your seam and hem allowance. Dashed lines are stitch lines; dart legs are dotted. Fold pieces have no allowance along the fold. Transfer grainlines, dart points, and notches.
  6. New sleeve styles. Bell sleeves have a wide hem. Puff sleeves include a cuff: gather the cap between notches to fit the armhole, then gather the sleeve hem to the cuff. Raglan sleeves have separate front/back halves: match RF and RB to the corresponding body edges; join the sleeve top seams and underarm seams. On raglan puff sleeves, fullness is gathered at the cuff. Square/V necklines need corner/miter finishing; neckline strips are starting lengths. Corset panels require main fabric, lining, fitted boning channels and a back closure; fit them in a toile before adding the skirt.
  7. New accessories. Corset sleeves attach to shoulder-supported panels; a cup corset uses a fitted foundation behind the cups. Choose a raised back and neckline, then sleeve shape and length. Trousers offer side-seam, slash and patch pockets with P1/P2 opening or placement marks. Peter Pan, stand and pointed shirt collars include shoulder marks and matching neck seams. Printed sewing notes explain assembly.
  8. Separate bust cups. Choose Corset, Separate bust cups, then Corset dress. Enter one cup root width, the horizontal measurement over the bust point, lower cup depth and coverage above the bust point. Sew lower inner to lower outer at C3; attach the upper cup at C1/C2. Insert cups into the corset cradle at C4/C5. The PDF includes sewing notes. Test cup volume and support in a toile.
  9. Bust dart length. The raw front side includes dart take-up. Sew the bust dart before matching front and back side seams; do not trim away the extra front length. Centre-front finished length remains as entered.
  10. Join princess panels. These are seams, not darts to fold. With right sides together, pin the front centre to front side along the long curved edges, matching F1 to F1, F2 to F2 and F3 to F3. Sew on the dashed stitch line using the selected seam allowance. Join back centre to back side at B1, B2 and B3. Ease the curves, clip seam allowances carefully after sewing, and press. The panels together form the complete armhole; then attach the sleeve. Make a toile first to check bust fit.
  11. Make a toile. These are original parametric starting drafts, informed by your references, not exact reproductions of a complete textbook method. Bust shaping, sleeve cap, crotch fit and style variations require a test fitting. Use a centre-back opening for bodices, dresses and skirts; a centre-front opening for trousers. T-shirts use stretch jersey and include a neckband. Sweatshirts use sweatshirt knit and include a neckband, cuffs and hem band. Bands use your chosen ribbing ratio; check stretch and recovery on your fabric. Facings, closures, waistband and pockets for woven garments are not included.

A4 = 210 × 297 mm. Print area = 210 × 297 mm, borderless. Use a borderless printer setting at 100% actual size. Profiles remain in this browser and are not uploaded.
